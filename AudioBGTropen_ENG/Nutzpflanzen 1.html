    Station 9 – Crop Plants 1

    Textversion

    We have now arrived at the last two stops on your journey. You are now in the first section of tropical useful plants. Cultivated plants are plants used by humans. These include not only food crops but also species that provide raw materials for technical applications. Many of these plants are native to the tropics but are now cultivated worldwide. For some of these tropical crops, it is no longer so easy to trace where their true home lies. In this greenhouse, you will find mainly plants used for technical purposes. A wellknown example is the rubber tree, Hevea basiliensis. Natural rubber, an important raw material for the production of rubber, is extracted from its milky sap, known as latex. You may notice cuts in the trunk. Latex oozes out of these and is collected. This method has been used for many centuries. The rubber tree is native to South America. Today, however, it is primarily cultivated in Southeast Asia. It is fascinating to note that as early as 1600 B.C., peoples of Central America and the Amazon were already using rubber, for example to make a solid rubber ball for a ritual ball game. This demonstrates how extensively humans have spread plants and altered their uses. In addition to rubber, other tropical plants also provide many other raw materials, such as fibers, dyes, or ingredients for medicines. Feel free to take a closer look here and notice just how diverse the potential uses of plants can be.

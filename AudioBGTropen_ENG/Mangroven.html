    Station 3 – Mangroves

    Textversion

    A few meters further into the lowland rainforest, you’ll pass a pond with mangrove plants. Mangroves grow in the tropics along coastlines. Their habitat is shaped by the tides, that is, by the regular alternation of high and low tide. As a result, the plants are exposed to fluctuating water levels, which are often associated with high salinity and varying temperatures. To survive under these conditions, mangroves have developed special adaptations. Particularly striking, for example, are their prop roots, which they use to anchor themselves in the soft, muddy soil. Dealing with salt also poses a challenge, but many mangroves can either actively excrete the salt or store it in their tissues and later shed it. Another unique feature is evident in their reproduction, as their seeds often germinate while still attached to the parent plant. This allows the young plants to develop directly and prevents them from being washed away by the tides immediately. These are referred to as viviparous plants. Feel free to take a closer look at the roots here; they give us a good insight into just how unique this habitat is.

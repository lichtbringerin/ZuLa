    Station 10 – Carnivorous Plants

    Textversion

    On your journey through the world of plants, you will encounter a special survival strategy here in this water basin: carnivorous plants. Carnivorous plants usually grow in nutrient-poor locations. They have developed a strategy to better obtain important minerals. They use insects in particular as a source of nutrients. Carnivores always use the same basic mechanism. First, the prey is attracted, then caught and digested, and finally the plant absorbs the released nutrients. However, the type of trap varies greatly. In snap traps, such as the well-known Venus flytrap (Dionaea muscipula), two leaf halves snap shut as soon as an insect touches them. Sticky traps, such as sundews (Drosera spec), have sticky glandular hairs that trap small insects. Pitcher or tube traps (Sarracenia) attract prey with a sweet scent and cause them to slip into a liquid from which most insects cannot escape. These different types of traps have developed independently of each other in various plant groups and on different continents over the course of evolution. This shows how effective this strategy can be in extreme habitats. However, carnivores are not exclusively dependent on meat, as they can also survive on nutrients obtained from other sources. A fascinating example of how diverse protective mechanisms developed through evolutionary processes can be.

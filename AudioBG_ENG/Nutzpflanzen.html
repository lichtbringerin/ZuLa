    Station 11 – Native Crops

    Textversion

    The beds in front of you focus on plants that are essential to our survival, even though we sometimes hardly notice them anymore: native crops. There is a wide variety of crops, and to give you a better overview, the plants on this terrace are divided into different groups. These include spices and herbs, vegetable plants, cereals, oil and protein plants, as well as starch and sugar suppliers. Dye and fibre plants also have their place here. In the past, these were used, for example, to dye fabrics or to make ropes and clothing. A large part of the beds are used to grow global crops such as wheat, barley, rye, millet, oats and maize. These cereals are essential for us humans and are among the staple foods in Germany. You can also discover an example of a native and very diverse crop here: cabbage. This has been further developed into numerous varieties through plant breeding. From white cabbage to Brussels sprouts to kale and broccoli. This diversity shows how intensively humans have used plants and changed them through the cultivation of wild plants. On the one hand, to increase yields, on the other hand to adapt them to the climate, soil and desired taste. You will encounter other useful plants in other areas of the garden, such as the medicinal garden, the cottage garden or the tropical greenhouse.

    Station 9 – Tropical Greenhouses

    Textversion

    You are now at the entrance to the tropical greenhouse. Your journey through the world of plants will take you on a tour of several greenhouse areas, through the perpetually humid tropics and the dry desert regions of the subtropics. The tropics extend around the equator in South and Central America, Africa, Southeast Asia and the Pacific region. Here, plants grow in climatic conditions that are hardly comparable to those in our latitudes: high temperatures, strong sunlight and, depending on the region, either extreme humidity or great dryness lead to a diverse and specially adapted flora. Unlike in Germany, for example, there are only two instead of four seasons here. The year is divided into rainy seasons and dry seasons. The tropical greenhouse is organised thematically. You can walk through it on a tour of different areas: in the lowland rainforest and mountain cloud forest, you will find plants in locations with abundant rainfall. In the lowland rainforest, temperatures are high all year round, while in the high altitudes of the mountain cloud forests it is significantly cooler. Feel the difference. Here you will find lianas and epiphytes, for example. In the next two greenhouse areas, so-called xerophytes are cultivated. These are plants that can survive on very little water. They originate from the dry regions of the tropics. The Palaeotropics in Africa and the Neotropics in South and Central America. These plants have developed many survival strategies: they store water in their thick leaves or stems, protect themselves with thorns or roll up their leaves to minimise water loss. In the narrow flower path that follows, the plant population changes regularly. Plants with unusual and striking flowers are on display. At the end of your tour, two greenhouses await you with a selection of well-known crops from the tropics and subtropics, such as cocoa, coffee, sugar cane and bananas. These are non-native plants that shape our everyday lives, either directly or through their products, but originate from distant regions. Immerse yourself in these exotic habitats and discover how diverse the world of tropical plants is.

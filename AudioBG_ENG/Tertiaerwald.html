    Station 2 – Tertiary Forest

    Textversion

    The Tertiary Forest offers you a fascinating insight into the flora of a long-gone geological era. The forest is an arboretum, i.e. the botanical garden's collection of trees and shrubs. The path through this section takes you across the meadow between groups of trees. Please note that the path is therefore only partially accessible. During the Tertiary period, approximately 65 to 2.5 million years ago, the climate in Central Europe was significantly warmer than it is today. This climate had a major impact on the flora. Many plant species that were native to our latitudes at that time no longer exist or they only occur in warmer regions. In the middle of the Tertiary Forest is the genus of Judas trees, Cercis. While many different species of this genus still existed in our region during the Tertiary period, most of those species are now only native to the Mediterranean region, South America and China. Here in the Tertiary Forest, you can discover some representatives of this genus from these areas. Cercis siliquastrum, Cercis canadensis and Cercis chinensis. The former biodiversity can be experienced here once again. A visit in spring is particularly worthwhile, as the Judas trees bloom bright pink in April. Take note of the flowers that emerge densely from the tree trunk. The Tertiary Forest helps us to better understand the development of our present-day flora, especially when it comes to seeding plants. It shows how closely climate and vegetation are interconnected and how flora and their habitats have changed over the course of Earth's history. Feel free to venture further into the Tertiary Forest and take a look around. You may even find one of the Judas tree species. Feel free to use the nature trail with its information boards to gain further exciting insights. It shows how strongly climate and vegetation are correlated.

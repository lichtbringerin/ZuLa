    Station 8 – Siebold

    Textversion

    Between the lecture hall and the administration building, you will find a hidden section. Here, the plant labels are marked with a red dot. But even when walking through the rest of the botanical garden, you will find some plant labels marked with a red dot. The dogs mark so-called Siebold plants and symbolises the Japanese flag. The physician and botanist Philipp Franz von Siebold was born in Wuerzburg in 1796. He completed his medical studies at our university. Siebold is considered one of the most important mediators between European and East Asian flora. In the 1820s, Siebold lived and worked in Japan, which was largely isolated from the rest of the world at the time. There he gathered knowledge about the country and its culture that were yet unknown by the western world. He also collected numerous plant species, many of which he brought to Europe for the first time and described them scientifically. Even today, his work, Flora Japonica, is still significant for botany. An important example is the paulownia, as this genus was named by Philipp Franz von Siebold and brought to Europe. The bluebell tree is a spectacular ornamental tree during its flowering season in May and is definitely worth a visit. As you continue your tour, keep an eye out for the red dots on the signs, which draw your attention to this remarkable Wuerzburg physician, ethnologist, naturalist and botanist. You may even discover some ornamental plants that are popular today but originally came from Japan.

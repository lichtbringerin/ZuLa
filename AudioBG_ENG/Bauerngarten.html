    Station 3 – Cottage Garden

    Textversion

    The cottage garden is a place where useful, beautiful and symbolic elements are closely intertwined. It is home to one of the greatest varieties of species in the botanical garden. Its origins lie in medieval monastery gardens, where medicinal plants, vegetables and ornamental plants were cultivated side by side. This approach was later continued in rural gardens, as a place of self-sufficiency and flourishing everyday life. Typical of a cottage garden is mixed planting, meaning that wild species, useful plants, ornamental plants and fruit trees get planted in the same beds. The classic cottage garden is structured by a crossroad that divides the area into four parts. In the centre there is traditionally a rose as a symbol of Mary. The cottage garden in the botanical garden in Wuerzburg is also divided into four different areas: one area with ornamental plants, one with wild plants and one with useful plants. In the fourth area, you can see the cottage garden with mixed planting in its classic form. Many of the plants here have been passed down through generations and been selected through careful observation and use. Also take note of the garden's boundary. The fence is woven from branches of shrubs, known as ‘Gerten’. This is not only practical as a boundary and for protecting the plants, but also points to the Indo-European origin of the word. Here, “Garten” means ‘enclosed place’. A tour of this section allows you to experience the huge variety of plants and, at the same time, the orderliness of a traditional cottage garden. At the back of the cottage garden, you will find the wild bee hotel. For more information about bees, please visit the bee pavilion. There is an audio guide station specifically about bees.

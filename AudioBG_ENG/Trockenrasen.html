    Station 16 – Dry Grasslands

    Textversion

    Semi-dry grasslands are an example of a cultural landscape with a close connection between nature and human use. These open, flower-rich meadows were created over centuries through extensive grazing with sheep and goats. They mainly developed on calcareous, dry and sunny slopes. They are highly biodiverse, which is why it is often possible to find well over 100 plant species in a very small area. Here in the Main Valley, such semi-dry grasslands are not uncommon. They are characterised by their great structural diversity. Low herbs, islands of grass and small copses all grow side by side. A typical representative of the semi-dry grassland is, for example, the upright brome grass (Bromus erectus). Semi-dry grasslands provide a suitable habitat not only for plants, but also for many insects and small animals. Native orchids, such as the so-called goat's beard, also grow on semi-dry grasslands. You can find them here in summer. Without maintenance, however, semi-dry grasslands would quickly become overgrown and eventually turn back into forest. Regular mowing or grazing, as with dry grasslands and sandy grasslands, ensures that this special form of cultural landscape is preserved. In the past, sheep herds were used for this purpose, but nowadays mowing is the main method. Not far from this section, you will find the Main-Franconian dry grassland, which occurs naturally north of Würzburg. Unlike semi-dry grassland, this is not a cultivated landscape, but a natural landscape. No maintenance is required for its preservation. It is assumed that no forest has developed at this location since the last ice age due to the extreme conditions.

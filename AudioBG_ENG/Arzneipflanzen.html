    Station 4 – Medicinal Garden

    Textversion

    A particularly exciting part of your journey through the world of plants awaits you here in the medicinal garden. Similar to the cottage garden or domestic crops, you will find plants here that we encounter every day. Some have been used since time immemorial to alleviate ailments and treat illnesses. Even today, many medicines are of plant origin. These are known as phytotherapeutics, i.e. medicines that contain parts of plants. The plants in the medicinal garden are arranged according to their main ingredients: alkaloids, essential oils, polyketides, terpenoids or phenylpropanes. Some beds also describe the plants’ uses, for example as a laxative or to support the cardiovascular system. And some of the species on display are also poisonous, but it is important to remember that the dose makes the poison. In the two rows of beds directly in front of you at the entrance, you will find wild mint species (Mentha spec) on the left.on the right, you will find peppermint (Mentha x piperita) and other hybrids of the wild species. True peppermint can be used to treat a wide variety of medical complaints. Whether for digestive problems, colds or headaches. Thanks to its essential oils, the plant is versatile and can also be used in the classic way, for example as peppermint tea. Feel free to use the scented herb bed. There, you can gently rub the leaves and smell the herbs to experience the world of medicinal plants with your other senses. You may even recognise mint here. The medicinal garden is also actively used for teaching purposes. Every year, pharmacy and biology students come here to learn about plants with active ingredients.

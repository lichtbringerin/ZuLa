    Station 14 – Mediterranean Rocky Heathlands

    Textversion

    This station accompanies you on your way through a special type of vegetation that is common in the Mediterranean region from Portugal to Asia Minor: Mediterranean rocky heathland. Please note that the middle part of the path is quite narrow and consequently not entirely accessible. The climate in Wuerzburg, with its hot, dry summers and comparatively mild winters, makes it possible to display this plant community in the open air, which is otherwise rare in Germany. Originally, the Mediterranean region, like Central Europe, was covered with dense, tall forests, mainly consisting of holm oaks. Due to intensive human use dating back to ancient times, these forests were constantly cleared and overexploited. Frequent fires caused by the dry summers also contributed to this. As a result replacement plant communities developed. The rocky heaths or thorny cushion heaths, as they are reproduced here, are one such plant community that has formed throughout the Mediterranean region as a result of the overuse of the original forests. The plants are adapted to extreme conditions such as direct sunlight, long periods of drought, nutrient-poor soils and often stony ground. Many species survive these conditions by developing spiny cushion plants. The spiny cushions not only protect them from predators such as goats or sheep, but also minimise evaporation and keep the soil under the plant moist. If you like, carefully place your hand on one of these thorny cushions. You will feel how firm, compact and resilient these plants are. Because of their resilience, the name “thorny cushion” is very apt. The Mediterranean rock heath is an example of how evolution has promoted certain forms and survival strategies in plants that are adapted to extremely difficult growing and living conditions. A small piece of Mediterranean vegetation in the middle of Wuerzburg. You can find more plants from the Mediterranean regions in the Mediterranean House and in the courtyard of the Tropical greenhouse.

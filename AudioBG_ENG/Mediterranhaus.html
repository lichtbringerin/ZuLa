    Station 5 – Mediterranean House

    Textversion

    The Mediterranean House is a section of your journey that takes you to one of the most speciesrich climate zones in the world. These regions are characterised by long, hot, dry summers and wet winters. For this reason, this climate zone is also called a winter rain climate. In order to survive here, plants must be able to cope with very little water for several months, especially in summer when it gets particularly hot. However, the Mediterranean climate is not only found around the Mediterranean Sea. Similar conditions also prevail in California, parts of Chile, South Africa and south-western Australia, everywhere between the 30th and 40th parallels. In all these regions, plant types with similar appearance have developed. This phenomenon is called convergent evolution. So what do typical plants in Mediterranean regions actually look like? Evergreen sclerophyllous plants are characteristic of these habitats. These are plants with small, often leathery leaves or needle-like structures. Many leaves are very hairy, often curled or shiny. All these adaptations reduce water loss and provide protection from predators. So, as you walk around, pay particular attention to the leaves of the plants.

    Station 15 – Sand Grasslands

    Textversion

    Sand grasslands are among the most extreme habitats in Central Europe and are also home to highly specialised plant species. These barren, sandy soils are created when loose sand is deposited by wind or water. Some such areas with sand deposits up to the height of one metre can be found along the Main and other large rivers. They were formed during and after the last ice age. As with the Main-Franconian dry grasslands, the vegetation is sparse, the soil hot and dry, and nutrients are scarce. This is a challenge for many plant species. However, plants that have adapted to these conditions can be found here. One example is the small silver grass (Corynephorus canescens) with needle-like leaves, which you can spot here and there if you look closely. Typical are small, drought-resistant species, that either have many roots close to the surface or have deep-reaching roots. Many plants have dense grey or white hairs to reduce evaporation. Sandy grasslands are home to many endangered plants. You can find these plants on the Red list because they are so rare they are threatened with extinction. One example of this would be the Sand Jurinea (Jurinea cyanoides). The Botanical Garden takes special care for this plant species. In order to preserve these rare habitats, maintenance is required, for example through grazing, to keep the sandy areas open. In this case, disturbances are even important, as they prevent scrub encroachment. Sandy grasslands have become rare, yet precisely because of their barrenness, they are places of diverse adaptations and therefore also habitats for endangered plant species.
